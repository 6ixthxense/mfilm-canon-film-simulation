# Shooting a paired shot

[ภาษาไทย](paired-shot.th.md) · [← Testing](../TESTING.md)

A **paired shot** is how a film stock gets measured against the real film: one roll of the film and the
same 24 frames on a Canon, with a colour chart in most of them. A lab scans the roll, M-Film develops
the Canon frames, and the chart's patches are compared. A film stock whose colour and tone come out
within the pass marks is **measured**; every other one is "in the style of" its film. The words in bold
are in the [glossary](../GLOSSARY.md).

No film stock is measured yet. The first roll is the maintainer's, Kodak Gold 200, and it rehearses this
page. Once that set is done, anyone who shoots film can offer a paired shot. The first film stocks are
Kodak Portra 400, Kodak Gold 200, Kodak Ektar 100, Fujichrome Velvia 50, CineStill 800T, Ilford HP5 Plus
and Fujifilm Pro 400H.

It takes one roll, about an hour of steady light, and a lab that will scan the roll twice.

## 1. What you need

- **The chart:** a ColorChecker Classic, 24 patches, full size (not the pocket one). The same chart
  goes in front of both cameras. Note when it was bought.
- **The film:** 135, fresh and in date, kept cool. Note the expiry date and the batch number on the
  box. A 24-exposure roll holds the list exactly; on a 36-exposure roll shoot the rest with the lens
  cap on. Nothing else goes on the roll.
- **The film camera:** manual exposure, an electronically timed shutter, and **shutter speeds in half
  stops**. Best is a Canon EOS film body with the EF lens that then goes on the Canon, so the lens adds
  no colour of its own.
- **The Canon:** any camera M-Film runs on, shooting RAW, with a roll of this film stock loaded in
  M-Film, so the camera is on the film stock's ISO and white balance. Don't change the white balance
  by hand. In Canon's menu set *Exposure level increments* to **1/2 stop**.
- A grey card that fills the frame, a tripod if you have one, and this page's card (section 4).

## 2. The light

- **A daylight film stock:** direct sun under a clear sky, between about 10:00 and 15:00. Turn the
  chart so the sun falls on it at about 45°, with no glare on the patches, away from coloured walls.
- **A tungsten film stock** (CineStill 800T): one tungsten-halogen lamp, about 3200 K, in a room with
  no other light. Not an LED.
- **The second light**, for frames 14 to 16: open shade under blue sky. For a tungsten film stock:
  direct sun.

The light must not change while you shoot. If a cloud comes, wait.

## 3. The exposure

- Both cameras: the film stock's ISO, f/8, the same shutter speed.
- **"0"** is the exposure at which the Canon's spot meter reads the chart's mid-grey patch as normal.
  That patch is in the bottom row, the fourth from the left. Go close until the patch fills the spot
  circle, set the shutter speed so the meter is in the middle, then step back.
- Every other exposure is counted from 0 **with the shutter speed, never the aperture**. One stop is
  two half-stop steps: +1 is two steps slower, −1 two steps faster, +0.5 one step slower.
  The half-stop speeds: 1/8000, 1/6000, 1/4000, 1/3000, 1/2000, 1/1500, 1/1000, 1/750, 1/500, 1/350,
  1/250, 1/180, 1/125, 1/90, 1/60, 1/45, 1/30, 1/20, 1/15.
- Write the 24 shutter speeds on the card before you start.

## 4. The 24 frames

Shoot each frame **on film first, then on the Canon**, within two minutes, from the same place, at the
same exposure. Put the chart in the middle of the frame, about a third of its width. No recognisable
person other than you may be in any frame: skin is your own hand and forearm.

- For frames 14 to 16, meter "0" again in the second light.
- For frames 17 to 22, "0" is what the camera's meter says for that scene.
- For a tungsten film stock, frames 17 to 22 are scenes lit by tungsten lamps, or shot at night.
- Frames 9 and 10 look far too bright. That is what they are for: the film still holds the chart there,
  and a Canon does not.
- Slide film has little room either side of 0. Shoot the same list.

What each frame is used for: the look is built from frames 1, 2, 3, 6 and 8. Frames 4, 5 and 7 are
held back and decide whether the film stock is measured. Frames 14 to 16, 9, 10 and 24 are measured and
reported. Frames 11 to 13 are for grain, 17 to 22 are looked at, and 23 is the bare film.

## 5. At the lab

Hand over all the rolls that are to be compared together, so they are developed in one batch. Ask for
two scans of each roll, on a **Noritsu HS-1800**. This is the note; hand it over or send it as it is:

> **Two scans of this roll, please, on the Noritsu HS-1800.**
>
> Develop it normally: the standard process, no push or pull.
>
> **Scan 1, locked.** Scene correction off (DSA, automatic contrast and colour). Set colour and density
> on frame 1 only, so that the chart's mid-grey patch reads neutral by the numbers on the screen, not by
> eye. Keep those values for the whole roll and touch no other frame. 16-bit uncompressed TIFF, the
> largest size, sRGB. Sharpening and grain suppression at your usual values; please tell me what they
> are.
>
> **Scan 2, automatic.** Your everyday corrected scan, as the largest JPEG.
>
> Please return the negatives uncut, in sleeves.

For black-and-white, ask which developer, dilution and time were used. A scan on another maker's
machine is welcome beside these two; it does not replace the Noritsu's.

Keep the negatives or slides flat, cool and dark: they may be scanned again.

## 6. What to hand over

- the locked scan's 24 TIFF files, and the automatic scan's JPEG files
- the Canon's 24 RAW files
- the **record**: copy [paired-shot-record.txt](paired-shot-record.txt) and fill it in. Every line
  has to have something written for it. The lines that say where the chart is in each picture, and
  the files' checksums, are added when the files arrive.

The maintainer's own go straight into the repository's releases. If you are not the maintainer, open
an issue and offer the files there, with this written in it:

> I shot these frames myself, and no one else's work or face is in them. M-Film may publish the scans
> and the Canon RAW files, and numbers computed from them, under CC BY 4.0, crediting me as ____ (or:
> without my name).

## 7. What makes a paired shot unusable

- other pictures on the roll, or frames missing from the list
- no locked scan, or a record that does not say which scanner and which settings
- a recognisable person other than the photographer in any frame
- the bracket made with the aperture, or with a camera that only has whole-stop shutter speeds
- the white balance changed by hand, or a light that changed during the roll
- an LED where a tungsten lamp is asked for
- film that is out of date (a discontinued film stock is the exception, and the record says so)

## 8. What happens next

The files are published with a release, and the record goes into
[`ml-dev/references`](../ml-dev/references/README.md). One command builds the film stock's look from
frames 1, 2, 3, 6 and 8, and another measures it on frames 4, 5 and 7. If it is within the pass marks,
the film stock is marked as measured on the shelf, with its numbers.
